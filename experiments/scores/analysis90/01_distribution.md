# 01 — The evaluation set: what was selected

Built from `artifacts/sets/eval5_heldout18` (90 blobs) and the five score files under `results/study_r122_eval90`. Every number below is regenerated by `make_analysis.py`.

## Design

- **Full factorial, 5 buildings × 18 weather windows = 90 scenarios.** Every building sees the same 18 windows, so a building effect and a weather effect can be separated.
- **All 90 are held out from training.** `building_train_3` and `building_train_6` were never in any training batch; the other three were trained on, but on different weather windows.
- **One deterministic 8,640-step month per cell** (5-min steps, 30 days). All 90 start **Sunday 06:00**; comfort is billing from Monday 06:00. No seed variance: a scenario has one trajectory, so every distribution below is across scenarios/time, not across runs.
- **Terms are already normalised** by the competition denominators (rated plant maxima), so energy and carbon are dimensionless fractions and comparable across buildings; comfort is the occupancy-weighted productivity regret.
- **Same setpoints for all five buildings** (day 21–24 °C, night 16–25 °C). What differs is geometry, envelope, plant count and occupancy.

`U` is the effective facade conductance implied by the calibrated envelope conductivity (`1/(0.1/k + 0.299)`); `env` is the volumetric envelope capacity. The two new buildings are the 70-zone and 43-zone rows: they sit in the middle and the upper-middle of the plant-size range, not at the extremes, so the set is not "small vs new".

- Action counts are the native gateway actions: 2 per zone (damper, reheat), 4 per AHU (run, supply-air temperature, static pressure, recirculation), 3 plant (run, water temperature, pump dP). The range is **75–209**; prior RL demos on this benchmark controlled 2 global setpoints.
- Occupancy differs by design: 43–170 people at full occupancy (1–2 per zone), which changes how much of the building the comfort term actually sees. The comfort bill applies on a fixed share of the month: 36% of the 8,640 steps carry any occupant in every building, and mean occupancy is 8.0–31.1 people.

- **Never trained on** is checked, not asserted: the training batches are `r85_four` (buildings 1, 2, 4, 5), `r96_light_four` (1, 4, 5, 7) and the shipped 30 (1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8). Buildings 3 and 6 appear in none of them.

**The set is not balance-weighted by station.** Minneapolis has 8 of the 18 windows, so 40 of the 90 scenarios and about half of the total score are Minneapolis. The window-weighted mean is therefore a Minneapolis-tilted number; the station-equal mean is reported beside it in file 02. This is a property of the selection, not of the policies.
- Buildings: all five have 18/90 = 20% of the scenarios. Windows: all 18 have 5/90 = 5.6%.
- Episode lengths: all 90 are 8,640 steps; all 90 start Sunday 06:00.

Spearman rank correlation between the per-scenario score and a set characteristic. Building-level variables (`zones`, `actions`, `u_eff`, `max_occ`) take only five distinct values and are strongly confounded with each other, so read them as description, not cause. The reading: for the two r122 saves a bigger, leakier building is harder (`zones` +0.57/+0.64, `u_eff` +0.56/+0.60), while `pid_water` and the r116 saves are nearly flat across building size (+0.02 to +0.15). Colder windows are harder for everyone (`hdd` +0.23 to +0.51) and hotter ones easier (`cdd` negative): the remaining cost is heating-side, not cooling-side.

## What this file adds beyond "building, weather"

- **Weather severity as numbers** (HDD/CDD/mean/swing), not just station names: the same station at two dates is two different problems, and the table shows it.
- **Plant size and action width together** (zones, AHUs, obs, 75–209 actions), which is what the control-scale claim needs; `FDM grid` is the solver's control-volume grid size, a proxy for how expensive the physics is, not a floor count.
- **Envelope physics** (effective U, envelope capacity, interior wall k) so "hard building" can be tied to a property rather than to its name.
- **Occupancy** (people per zone, max occupancy), because the comfort term is occupancy-weighted and an empty building has no comfort bill.
- **Balance accounting** (share of scenarios and share of score by station), which quantifies the Minneapolis dominance instead of hiding it.
- **The distribution of the policy scores themselves** (quantiles, per building, per station), so the headline mean has a spread behind it.
- **Full-factorial pairing**, which file 02 uses to split the variance into building, weather and interaction.
